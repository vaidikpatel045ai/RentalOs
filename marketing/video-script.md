# Bridal Rental OS — 60-Second Premium Marketing Video

Reference script/prompt for an external video-generation tool (e.g. Sora, Runway, Veo). This was authored as a full production brief and is preserved here verbatim, unmodified, for future use when producing the actual video asset.

---

## Video Style

Create a premium cinematic SaaS marketing film for a luxury bridal rental management platform called **Bridal Rental OS**.

**Target market:**
- Dubai, UAE
- Luxury bridal boutiques
- Bridal gown rental businesses
- Formalwear and occasion-wear rental businesses

**Visual style:**
- Luxury Dubai aesthetic
- Premium fashion campaign
- Cinematic lighting
- Elegant bridal boutique interiors
- Ivory, champagne, warm beige, soft black and subtle gold accents
- Modern SaaS UI
- High-end commercial production
- Smooth camera movement
- Sophisticated typography
- Minimal visual clutter
- Photorealistic
- Premium editorial fashion photography aesthetic

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9
**Duration:** 60 seconds

**Music:** Elegant cinematic electronic music with subtle Middle Eastern influence. Start calm and slightly tense, build energy, then become confident and uplifting.

**Voice:** Professional female or male narrator with a sophisticated international English accent. Calm, confident, premium.

Do NOT make the video look like a generic AI-generated advertisement.

---

## Scene 1 — The Chaos (0–7s)

**Visual:** Open inside a busy luxury bridal boutique in Dubai. Multiple beautiful bridal gowns hanging in a premium showroom. A salesperson is switching between WhatsApp messages, paper notes, spreadsheets, calendar, phone calls. Show rapid cinematic cuts. A bride is waiting for her fitting. A tailor is asking: "Which dress is this for?" A cleaner is holding a gown and checking a handwritten note. A staff member looks confused while checking a booking calendar. Show subtle visual tension.

**On-screen text:** "One boutique." *(pause)* "Too many moving parts."

**Voiceover:** "Running a bridal rental business means managing much more than dresses."

## Scene 2 — The Real Problem (7–14s)

**Visual:** Fast cinematic montage. A gown shown with overlapping labels: BOOKED / ALTERATION / CLEANING / RETURN / NEXT BOOKING. Calendar shows two bookings dangerously close together. Owner looks at the screen. Warning appears: "Potential booking conflict." Then: "Cleaning turnaround: 8 hours" / "Next booking: Tomorrow"

**Voiceover:** "Bookings, fittings, alterations, cleaning, payments, deliveries and returns — all happening at once."

**On-screen text:** "Where is the dress?" then "What's next?"

## Scene 3 — Introduce the Product (14–20s)

**Visual:** The chaos freezes. Fades to clean dark background. Premium interface appears. Logo: BRIDAL RENTAL OS. Tagline: "One system. Every garment. Every workflow." Interface smoothly expands into the dashboard.

**Voiceover:** "Meet Bridal Rental OS." *(pause)* "The operating system built for bridal and formalwear rental businesses."

## Scene 4 — Owner Dashboard (20–28s)

**Visual:** Dashboard cards — Today's Appointments (12), Trials (8), Pickups (6), Returns (4), Alterations Due (11), Cleaning (7), Pending Payments (AED 32,400), Deposits Held (AED 185,000). **Voiceover:** "See your entire operation at a glance — and know when a booking is truly safe."

## Scene 5 — Garment Lifecycle (28–35s)

**Visual:** Premium bridal gown, label BR-102. Camera transitions through: BOOKED → FITTING → TAILORING → READY → PICKUP → RETURN → INSPECTION → CLEANING → QUALITY CHECK → AVAILABLE. QR code on garment tag; scan opens the complete garment profile. Display: "23 Rentals", "AED 276,000 Revenue", "ROI 3.68×", "Condition 9.2/10".

**Voiceover:** "Track every garment from first fitting to final return — and every step in between."

## Scene 6 — Tailor Portal (35–40s)

**Visual:** Tailor working in a beautiful alteration room. Tablet screen: "MY TASKS TODAY" — Sarah Ahmed, BR-102, Hem -1.5", Bust +0.5", Straps -0.5", Due: 18 Sep, Priority: HIGH. Tailor taps START WORK, then READY FOR FITTING.

## Scene 7 — Cleaner/Washer Portal (40–45s)

**Visual:** Professional garment-care room. Cleaner scans the gown. Screen: CLEANING QUEUE — BR-102, Cleaning: Delicate Dry Clean, Stain: Sleeve, Status: CLEANING → QUALITY CHECK → READY. Before/after photos appear.

**Voiceover:** "Your cleaning team gets their own simple workflow."

## Scene 9 — Business Intelligence (51–56s)

**Visual:** Return to the luxury Dubai bridal boutique. Owner stands confidently while the boutique operates smoothly. Cut between bride, tailor, cleaner, delivery, garments, dashboard — everything synchronized. Final screen: BRIDAL RENTAL OS — "From fitting to return. Everything connected." CTA: "Built for modern bridal boutiques." Secondary CTA: "Book a Demo". Website placeholder: www.yourdomain.com

**Voiceover:** "Bridal Rental OS." *(pause)* "Every garment. Every bride. Every workflow. One system."

## Visual Generation Master Prompt

Create a cinematic 60-second premium SaaS marketing video for "Bridal Rental OS", an enterprise software platform designed for luxury bridal and formalwear rental boutiques in Dubai and the UAE.

Show an upscale Dubai bridal boutique with elegant architecture, luxury bridal gowns, sophisticated interior design, diverse professional staff, and brides receiving high-end personalized service.

The video should visually demonstrate the complete garment lifecycle: customer appointment → dress selection → booking → measurements → tailoring → fitting → ready → pickup/delivery → wedding → return → damage inspection → cleaning → quality control → available again.

Show the software dashboard as a premium modern SaaS interface.

**Important UI modules:** Owner dashboard, Inventory, Garment profile, Booking calendar, Appointment management, Customer CRM, Measurements, Tailor portal, Cleaner/washer portal, Delivery portal, Payments, Deposits, Damage inspection, Cleaning workflow, Analytics, Garment ROI, Dead-stock intelligence, Notifications, WhatsApp communication.

The UI should feel like a combination of a premium fashion-tech platform and a modern enterprise SaaS application.

**Design language:** Luxury minimalism. Warm ivory. Champagne. Soft beige. Dark charcoal. Subtle metallic-gold accents. Large elegant typography. Rounded modern interface components. Clean charts. Sophisticated tables. Beautiful garment photography. Use realistic AED pricing. Use realistic UAE names and Dubai locations. Show Arabic-ready interface styling without making the entire UI Arabic. Include subtle Dubai visual references such as modern luxury architecture and city atmosphere, but do not overuse recognizable landmarks. The software must always look like a real working product rather than fake futuristic holographic technology. Use realistic smartphone, tablet and desktop interfaces.

Make the interactions believable: clicking buttons, opening garment records, changing statuses, scanning QR codes, assigning tasks, updating cleaning status, updating tailoring status, confirming deliveries, checking booking availability.

**Camera:** slow cinematic dolly shots, smooth tracking shots, macro garment shots, premium fashion close-ups, controlled transitions, subtle depth of field, professional commercial cinematography.

**Lighting:** soft luxury showroom lighting, warm natural light, cinematic highlights, realistic skin tones, high dynamic range. No excessive neon. No cyberpunk. No cheap stock-video appearance. No cheesy corporate animations. No generic warehouse. No random clothing racks. No unrelated technology.

**Secondary tagline:** "From fitting to return. Everything connected."

**CTA:** "Book a Demo"

---

## Important Video Generation Rules

- Keep the same fictional product interface, logo and visual identity throughout the entire video.
- Keep garment IDs consistent — BR-102 should remain the same bridal gown throughout the lifecycle scenes.
- Keep customer Sarah Ahmed consistent.
- Keep the dashboard design consistent.
- Avoid changing UI layouts randomly between scenes.
- All on-screen text must be clean, correctly spelled and legible. Do not generate gibberish text.
- Do not use fake logos of third-party companies.
- Do not use recognizable real customer information.
- Do not show unnecessary people looking directly into the camera.
- The video should communicate premium reliability, operational control and modern technology.

**Final emotional message:** "Your boutique is complicated. Your software shouldn't be."
